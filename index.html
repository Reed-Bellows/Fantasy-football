Big Board

How the projections work

Each player's projection is opportunity times efficiency. Opportunity is his share of his team's targets, carries or pass attempts. Efficiency is what he does with them: catch rate, yards per target or carry, and touchdown rate.

Recent games count most, and last season counts less than this one. Every rate is pulled toward the average for his position, so a few big games can't swing it too far. Touchdown rates are pulled hardest, because touchdowns are the most random part of scoring.

Rest of season

The total adds up each remaining game's projection. Future games are discounted for the chance of injury: running backs play about 90% of them, receivers and tight ends 93%, quarterbacks 95%. Players on injured reserve are left out for at least four weeks.

The FLEX rank sorts running backs, receivers and tight ends by projected points. The value column shows projected points above a replacement-level starter at the same position (QB12, RB30, WR36, TE14, K12 and DST12 in a 12-team, one-QB league).

Team context

Betting lines set each team's expected points and game script: favorites run more, underdogs throw more, and high-scoring games create more touchdowns.

Matchups adjust yards and touchdowns by up to 10%, based on the points each defense has allowed to the position.

How the weekly rank is built

Weekly rankings are positional, the way start/sit decisions are made. Pick a position to see QB1, RB1 and so on, or FLEX to compare running backs, receivers and tight ends on one list.

Projections assume the player suits up. Check the injury tag before you set your lineup. When a teammate is out, his share of targets and carries is spread across the players who are left.

Range

The bar shows the middle 60% of likely outcomes (20th to 80th percentile), and the tick marks the projection. It's based on how much the player's scoring has swung from game to game, blended with what's typical for his position.

Scoring

Offense: 0.04 per passing yard, 4 per passing TD, −2 per interception, 0.1 per rushing or receiving yard, 6 per rushing or receiving TD, −2 per fumble lost, and 1, 0.5 or 0 per catch.

Kickers: 3 for a field goal under 40 yards, 4 from 40 to 49, 5 from 50 and up, 1 per extra point, −1 per miss. Defenses: 1 per sack, 2 per interception or fumble recovery, 6 per TD, 2 per safety, and 10 down to −4 for points allowed.

Projections are this site's own model. Stats, schedules, betting lines, rosters and injury reports come from nflverse (CC BY 4.0). Expected touchdowns come from the ffopportunity model.
